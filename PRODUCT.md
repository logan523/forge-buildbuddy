## Users

Two primary audiences, served through progressive disclosure:

**Weekend makers & hobbyists** — Building at a workbench, hands might be dirty, phone or laptop nearby. They want confidence they're not missing a part or making a dangerous mistake. Excited but sometimes intimidated. They value speed: surface-level guidance that gets them building fast.

**Engineering students & learners** — Using it alongside coursework or self-study. They want to understand WHY not just WHAT. Value explanations, skill progression, and budget-conscious sourcing. They drill deeper into the details.

Progressive disclosure handles both: Quick Start (surface, fast) for hobbyists, Step-by-Step with explanations for learners, Reference (schematics, full BOM, source code) for both.

## Product Purpose

BuildBuddy turns a YouTube link or text description into a complete, buildable hardware plan. AI extracts every part with real pricing and shopping links, generates step-by-step instructions with verification checkpoints, and guides makers from idea to working prototype. The platform makes hardware accessible without dumbing it down.

Success: a user goes from "I saw this cool project on YouTube" to "I built it and it works" in a single session, without searching for parts across 5 websites or guessing at missing steps.

## Brand Personality

**Workshop mentor.** Competent, warm, precise. Like a skilled friend in your workshop who knows exactly what to do and doesn't over-explain. Trustworthy expertise. The tone is respectful and precise — makers are capable, and the platform treats them that way.

Three words: **competent, warm, precise.**

The interface should feel crafted and intentional, like a well-organized workbench where everything is exactly where you need it. Premium without feeling fragile. Technical without feeling cold.

**Reference:** Craft / Things 3 — warm, textured, human. Paper-like surfaces, depth through shadow not borders. Feels crafted, not generated. Darker and more focused than Notion.

## Anti-references

This should NOT look like a children's STEM toy site. No cartoon illustrations, no primary colors, no over-simplified language. Makers are capable adults. The tone is respectful and precise.

Also avoid: generic SaaS dashboards (no blue buttons, no data tables with pagination, no "Welcome back, User"), cluttered marketplaces (no banner ads, no "customers also bought", no aggressive upsells), and AI-generated slop (no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no hero-metric templates).

## Design Principles

1. **Trust through precision.** Every part number, every pin label, every shopping link is correct or clearly flagged as uncertain. The platform earns trust by being honest about what it knows and what it doesn't.

2. **Show, don't tell.** Wiring diagrams, pinout tables, verification checkpoints, video frame references. Visual clarity over walls of text. A builder at a workbench should be able to glance and understand.

3. **Respect the maker's capability.** No hand-holding, no cartoons, no condescending language. Assume competence. Provide depth for those who want it, speed for those who don't.

4. **Progressive depth.** Surface for speed, drill down for understanding. Quick Start → Step-by-Step → Reference. Never trap an expert in beginner mode, never abandon a beginner in expert territory.

5. **Safety is non-negotiable.** Li-ion warnings, mains voltage flags, wire gauge validation. The platform actively prevents dangerous builds. Safety isn't a checkbox — it's a hardcoded layer that the AI cannot override.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast, keyboard navigation, screen reader support. Standard best practices done well. No specific accommodations beyond the basics.
